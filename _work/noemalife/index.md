## A suite of products

The design was based on real clinical use, not on desktop screens. Because needs change between the ward and the workstation, the mobile apps focused on what mobility helps most: quick checks, alerts, approvals, and updates at the point of care.

Clear information hierarchy guided the UI. Each screen supported one main task, with extra detail available when needed. Readable typography and spacing highlighted critical values, and simple, forgiving interactions helped prevent errors.

### Galileo

Galileo is hospital software that brings electronic medical records, lab data, and diagnostic exams together into a single digital system. It helps clinicians share information quickly and deliver safer care.

It’s a suite of interconnected applications (or modules) that work together on the same clinical platform. Each department uses the tools it needs — such as apps for the Emergency Department, Radiology, and the Clinical Laboratory — but everything shares the same patient record and data model.

- **fewer errors:** no information gets lost or recorded incorrectly.
- **time savings:** nurses and doctors don’t need to hunt down paper files in archives.

## Consistency across a complex product family

Because Noemalife’s mobile apps were built on different clinical systems, consistency was essential. I used the same patterns for navigation, lists, detail screens, and status indicators so clinicians could move between tools without learning a new interface each time.

This also made the apps easier to maintain and extend. Reusable components meant we could add new features and new data without redesigning every screen, keeping the whole product family consistent as it evolved.
